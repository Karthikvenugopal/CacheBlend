> Follow-up: [the bounded diagnosis](selector-diagnosis.md) found no implementation or tie-breaking fault. The signals differ at token level but share all pilot region rankings. The rejection and inconclusive factual-efficacy conclusion remain unchanged.

# Feasibility decision

**Reject the current first-layer delta selector as tested; retain the validated numerical update.** It selected exactly the same 32 downstream repair positions as local repair and attention-to-edit on all 24 held-out questions. Six questions have only two eligible regions, making their selection forced; the remaining 18 questions offer 8–19 regions, and delta still selected the same local pair on all 18. It therefore produced identical logits and answers to those controls while requiring more metadata and selection work. This rejects continuing with this particular selector unchanged on this regime, not every possible edit-repair mechanism or every possible use of the numerical identity.

Gate 1 passed six native-model cases, with maximum absolute head-output error 5.96e-8 and no observed fallback. Metadata is original-clean first-layer Q/O in float32 plus per-head log-normalizers in float64: 16.25 KiB/token. Collection requires an extra chunked score pass in this implementation. Full details and timings are in [Gate 1](feasibility-gate1.md).

Gate 2 completed eight development questions and 24 separate held-out questions, using frozen source/config/data. The held-out questions share eight document edits, so they are not 24 independent samples. Actual document lengths are 75–335 tokens; derived evidence distances are 3, 94 or 259 tokens. The original two pre-pause cases were preserved; 22 unfinished cases were resumed without tuning or changing inference source.

**Factual improvement is inconclusive with this model and fixed prompting/12-token generation protocol.** Original clean outputs supplied 15/24 required answers; edited full recomputation supplied 13/24. Edited full answers were correct on 8/8 direct questions, 0/8 derived questions and 5/8 invariant questions. All methods, including edit-only and the three random controls, obtained the same 13/24 known-answer score and preserved the 13 clean-correct answers. That is not evidence that the selectors repaired the derived dependencies: the clean reference could not solve those cases.

A manual audit distinguishes real wrong values and missing/ambiguous answers from formatting. Of the eleven unsuccessful edited-full outputs, three give the wrong requested value or table row (the basic/10-credit row instead of the plus/20-credit allowance, morning instead of afternoon, Rome instead of Paris), five do not complete the derived answer within the limit, and three do not answer the invariant question. None of these eleven was merely a numeric spelling mismatch. The 13 successful factual answers are verbose, explaining why strict short-answer format scores are zero. The [manual annotations](../artifacts/feasibility/manual-factual-audit.json) remain separate from the unchanged frozen scores. Longer generation or a stronger model might change these findings; neither was tried after freezing.

Distribution fidelity and answer utility tell different stories. Delta/local/attention had identical median next-token KL of 0.001098 and greedy-token agreement on 16/24 questions. Edit-only had median KL 0.003159. Random seeds varied: their median KL values were 0.000679, 0.001567 and 0.001483. No selector changed the factual score. We did not choose a random seed after seeing results or claim statistical significance from this small correlated pilot.

Measured median selection times were 2.14 ms for delta, 0.22 ms for attention-to-edit and 0.011 ms for local repair. Attention-to-edit retains 8.25 KiB/token; local needs neither set of attention metadata. Median original-prefill-plus-metadata collection was 416.16 ms for delta and 409.67 ms for attention, compared with 408.19 ms for uninstrumented original prefill. These single per-case preparation measurements are noisy; positive postprocessing costs and repeated Gate 1 observations are also preserved.

Including tokenization, selection, copying, repair, fresh question and generation, measured median resume times were 1.189 s for full recomputation and 1.095 s for delta. Adding original cache construction and method-specific metadata collection gives 1.597 s and 1.556 s respectively. Those functional observations span two sessions, use two repetitions and include differing generated continuations. They do not establish a reliable speedup. In particular, the small end-to-end difference is not proportional to the large difference in recomputed-token counts. Delta versus local timing differences are measurement variability around the same actual repair set, not evidence for a better selector.

The next method iteration would need a signal that changes useful repair choices beyond this local ordering, and an evaluation in which clean recomputation can solve the dependent questions. This experiment supplies neither evidence for scaling the unchanged selector nor a novelty claim. No further method, model download, training, cloud compute or benchmark expansion was started.
